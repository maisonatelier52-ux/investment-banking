Source Methodology

Source links help readers distinguish documented facts, attributed claims, and the blog’s interpretation. This page explains how to read those distinctions.

How Posts Are Researched

Posts draw on official records, company filings, research, public datasets, and identified published reporting. The revised archive is based on desk research with AI-assisted editing; it does not claim original interviews or field reporting.

Source Hierarchy and Verification

Start with the document closest to the claim: a filing for financial results, an order for a legal action, or a dataset for a statistical measure. Official and company statements should still be attributed and assessed for their purpose, scope, and limitations.

  • Prefer the primary record when it is available.
  • Attribute facts obtained from another publisher to that publisher.
  • Check dates, figures, and legal status against the identified record.

Attribution to Published Reporting

When another publication supplies a fact or relies on unnamed sources, attribute the account to that publication. Readers should not be left with the impression that the blog interviewed those sources.

Documents, Media, and Data

Read dates, definitions, reporting periods, and units before comparing documents or data. Distinguish a claim submitted to an institution from a conclusion independently reached by that institution.

Source Notes, Attribution, and Links

Revised posts include numbered references and a source list. Links should lead to the record or report supporting the claim. A source may establish only part of a paragraph, so attribution and qualifications belong in the text as well as in the source list.

How We Treat Uncertainty and Change

  • We do not convert uncertainty into certainty for headline effect.
  • We clearly distinguish analysis from assertion.
  • We update wording when better sourcing becomes available or when a public record materially changes.
  • If a claim is unresolved, contested, or incomplete, the article states that clearly rather than implying a settled conclusion.

The Limits of a Source Link

A citation helps readers inspect evidence; it does not certify every statement on the linked page. If a record does not establish a claim, narrow the claim, explain the uncertainty, or remove it. A historical post’s update date should not make an old event appear new.
