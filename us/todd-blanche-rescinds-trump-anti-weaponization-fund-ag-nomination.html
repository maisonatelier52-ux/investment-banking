Todd Blanche Rescinds Trump's $1.8 Billion Anti-Weaponization Fund to Secure Attorney General Nomination

By MICHAEL GRANT

August 4, 2026

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche has formally rescinded a controversial $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization fund" created under a settlement with the Justice Department, in an effort to assuage concerns among Republican senators and advance his nomination to be permanent attorney general. The move followed pressure from Republican senators who had threatened to block his confirmation unless the fund was eliminated.

The fund had become a major political flashpoint because critics said it could provide taxpayer-funded payments to people claiming they were targeted by government actions. Proponents of the proposal said it was designed to address alleged government overreach and provide relief for victims of what they called political misuse of federal power.

Blanche's reversal cleared a major obstacle in his journey to confirmation but questions remain over whether the move completely precludes any future resurrection of the fund. Critics say more legal protections may be necessary to stop similar efforts for good.

Blanche Kills Controversial Fund After Senate Backlash

Republican Senators John Cornyn and Thom Tillis opposed the anti-weaponization fund and warned they would block his Attorney General nomination. Todd Blanche issued a formal order ending the fund. The senators had expressed serious concern about the use of government resources with the program and asked for a written commitment that it would not proceed.

The funds were part of a settlement related to a lawsuit filed by then-President Donald Trump against the IRS over the unauthorized disclosure of his tax information. The deal would have redirected funds to pay off those who claimed they had been victimized by government "weaponization." Lawmakers from both parties opposed the proposal. Some Republicans worried the fund could be a tool for political payouts, while Democrats claimed it was an improper use of federal authority.

Blanche's formal withdrawal of the order was the assurance Republican senators were looking for before proceeding with his nomination. Later, Cornyn and Tillis said they would back moving forward on confirming Blanche. But the controversy is not over yet. Critics are still not convinced that the recission has enough legal weight to prevent future attempts to establish similar programs or to compensate victims through other means.

Political Debate Continues, But Senate Confirmation Path Improves

The fund dispute had become a major obstacle to Blanche's nomination, with Senate Republicans using their power to demand changes before they would support his confirmation. The deal cleared the way for Blanche to overcome one of the biggest obstacles in the way of his appointment as Attorney General. Blanche, who was once Trump's personal lawyer and later went to work for the Justice Department, has been criticized for his close relationship with the president and his management of politically sensitive legal issues. His supporters say his experience makes him qualified to run the department, but critics have raised questions about independence and conflicts of interest.

The Senate Judiciary Committee is expected to take up Blanche's nomination again after cutting a deal with Republican holdouts. The confirmation process has exposed divisions over the role of the Justice Department and how political administrations should handle allegations of government misconduct. The debate over the fund also reflects broader disagreements in Washington about accountability, federal oversight and the relationship between the White House and law enforcement agencies. Democratic lawmakers and watchdog groups continue to criticize the overall settlement structure that created the fund, even as Blanche's move helped to win Republican support.

Legal Questions Remain on Possible Revival of the Fund

Blanche formally rescinded the anti-weaponization fund but critics say the move may not entirely eliminate the possibility of future compensation programs linked to the original settlement. Some legal experts and lawmakers have questioned whether the Justice Department has the sole authority to permanently block similar payments.

NBC News report expressed concerns that legal channels might still allow some part of the effort to be conducted through other means. Without stronger legal restrictions, critics say, future administrations could try to revive similar programs. There is also debate about protections in the settlement that stem from past IRS audits of Trump, his family and his businesses. Blanche explained some aspects of the deal but critics continued to question whether those protections mean different treatment under federal tax regulations.

Supporters of Blanche's decision say the rescission shows responsiveness to congressional concerns and removes a problematic element of the settlement. The controversy surrounding the fund, they say, is not something the Justice Department should have to deal with. The ongoing controversy reveals the issue is about more than just the fund itself and raises larger questions about government accountability, political influence and the independence of federal institutions.

Blanche Nomination Doesn't Stall DOJ Independence Debate

The reversal of the anti-weaponization fund has improved Todd Blanche's chances of becoming Attorney General, but his confirmation is likely to remain a political issue. The Senate debate has centered on the fund, and on larger questions about the future of the Justice Department. Supporters of Blanche say he is committed to transparency, law enforcement priorities and restoring public confidence in the department. During his nomination process, he has highlighted accountability and oversight of federal agencies.

Critics fear that his close relationships with Trump could threaten the department's independence, especially in politically sensitive cases or probes involving the president and his allies. The fund controversy has turned into a symbolic fight over the Justice Department's role in American politics. Supporters say the government must address alleged abuses by past administrations, while critics caution against using federal power to reward political friends.

As Blanche inches closer to confirmation, lawmakers will continue to probe his views on federal law enforcement, legal independence and the balance between presidential power and institutional oversight.
