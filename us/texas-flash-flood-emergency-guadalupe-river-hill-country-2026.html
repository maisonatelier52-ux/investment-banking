Hill Country flood safety: how to use warnings and river information

By MICHAEL GRANT

First published July 17, 2026 · Updated September 17, 2026

For travel in Texas Hill Country, check current flood warnings and local road information before setting out. Conditions at a river crossing can change quickly and may differ from conditions where rain is falling. This guide explains what to check; it does not report a current emergency.

Use a current warning for your location

Check the National Weather Service and local emergency-management instructions. Pay attention to the warning area and expiration time. A river observation from another location or an earlier hour may not describe the road or property in front of you.

If officials direct evacuation or movement to higher ground, follow the current instructions. Do not rely on an archived news page to decide whether a route is safe.

Keep vehicles out of floodwater

The National Weather Service advises drivers not to enter flooded roads. Water can conceal damage to the road surface, and its depth or force may be difficult to judge. Barriers and closures should be respected even if a route is familiar. [1]

Prepare before an alert

Identify a way to receive warnings, a safer destination, and routes that avoid low crossings. Consider the needs of children, older people, pets, and anyone requiring assistance. Conditions can change after a previous check, so continue following official updates during severe weather.

Recheck conditions before departure and when warnings change. A familiar route or an earlier clear crossing is not evidence that a road remains safe. Follow current official instructions and keep both vehicles and pedestrians out of floodwater. [1]
