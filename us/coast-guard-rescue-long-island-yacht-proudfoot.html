The Proudfoot rescue: what the Coast Guard’s April 2026 account shows

By MICHAEL GRANT

First published April 6, 2026 · Updated September 17, 2026

A Coast Guard helicopter crew rescued four people from the disabled motor yacht Proudfoot near Moriches Inlet, New York, on April 4, 2026. The agency’s April 5 release identified the vessel as 42 feet long and placed it about three miles southwest of the inlet. The rescued people were taken to Gabreski Airport for medical evaluation, with no injuries reported. [1]

What the official account establishes

The Coast Guard’s release and video document an MH-60T Jayhawk crew from Air Station Cape Cod carrying out a helicopter hoist. The record identifies the response and outcome, while the cause of the vessel’s loss of propulsion is a separate question.

The absence of reported injuries does not make the incident trivial. It also does not establish why the vessel became disabled or who was responsible for the failure.

Why disabled vessels can become difficult rescues

A loss of propulsion reduces a crew’s ability to maintain position or avoid hazards. Sea conditions, location, communications, and the number of people aboard influence the response. A successful rescue does not mean the same response would be available under every set of conditions.

What readers should take away

The case illustrates the importance of location, communications, and conditions in shaping a rescue. For planning a future trip, use current marine conditions and official boating guidance. The successful outcome here does not establish that the same response would be possible in different weather or at another location.
