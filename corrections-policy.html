Corrections Policy

Factual accuracy matters in every blog post. Readers can report errors with supporting evidence, and material corrections should explain what changed and why. An update date identifies a revision rather than a newly published event.

How We Handle Mistakes

Different types of errors require distinct responses:

Minor errors

Spelling, grammar, and typographical errors can be corrected without a separate note when the meaning is unchanged.

Factual errors

Correct material errors in names, figures, dates, and other factual claims on the affected post. A revision or correction note should explain what changed and why.

Later developments

A dated case study may be revised when new evidence changes its account. Identify the new development and its date. An editorial update does not imply that the post covers every subsequent event.

Where Corrections Appear

Place a material correction on the affected post, where readers can see it alongside the revised account.

Use a correction for an error, a clarification for wording that could mislead, and an update for a later development.

What a Correction Request Should Include

A useful correction request includes:

  • The article URL or headline
  • The specific line or claim you believe is incorrect
  • The factual basis for your objection
  • Supporting documents or dated sources

Reader Submissions

Send a correction request with a direct link and a short explanation of the disputed claim. An official document, dataset, or dated source helps the reviewer assess it.

Corrections Email: corrections@investment-banking.org

Our Commitment to Transparency

Material factual changes should remain visible through a note. Disagreement with an opinion is different from a factual error, but evidence that undermines the argument’s factual basis should be reviewed.

Why This Matters

Clear corrections let readers understand both the current account and why it changed.
