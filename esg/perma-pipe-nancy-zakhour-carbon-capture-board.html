Perma-Pipe’s board appointments: evaluating expertise and oversight

By Marcus D. Reynolds

First published April 8, 2026 · Updated September 17, 2026

Perma-Pipe International Holdings announced the appointment of Nancy Zakhour and Saleh Sagr to its board, effective April 8, 2026. Its April 7 release described Zakhour as an independent director and assigned her to the audit, compensation, and nominating and corporate governance committees. The company’s securities filing documents the appointment and independence determination. [1][2]

What the experience can contribute

The company describes Zakhour’s background as spanning energy and investment banking. Such experience can help a board question project assumptions, financing choices, and commercial risks. It does not establish that the company has secured a carbon-capture contract or that future projects will earn an attractive return.

Perma-Pipe supplies pre-insulated piping and leak-detection systems. Opportunities in energy infrastructure must still be assessed against the specifications, costs, and execution requirements of individual projects. A board appointment is not a substitute for that analysis.

Governance is more than a biography

Independence, committee responsibilities, relevant expertise, and oversight of management are different elements of board effectiveness. The filing is useful because it identifies formal responsibilities rather than relying solely on praise in a press release.

What investors can monitor

Follow the company’s accounts, backlog, margins, and capital allocation. Compare the experience a director brings with the decisions that director is responsible for overseeing. Board effectiveness becomes visible through the quality of those decisions and the company’s response when projects encounter problems.
